Slipreel vs QuickTime: common questions

Is Slipreel better than QuickTime for screen recording?

For polished demos, yes. Slipreel adds automatic zoom, cursor smoothing, device frames, captions, system-audio capture, and MP4 or GIF export that QuickTime does not have. For a quick raw capture you won't edit, QuickTime is fine and free.

Can Slipreel record system audio when QuickTime can't?

Yes. Slipreel captures system audio as its own track. QuickTime needs extra third-party software to record internal sound.

Does Slipreel export GIFs when QuickTime doesn't?

Yes. Slipreel exports both MP4 and GIF, in six aspect ratios. QuickTime exports .mov video only.

Is Slipreel free like QuickTime?

Slipreel is free to download and fully functional to record and edit; export requires a license ($12/month, $89/year, or $129 once). QuickTime is entirely free and built into macOS.

Does Slipreel add automatic zoom to a recording?

Yes. Slipreel detects your clicks and zooms in on them automatically, and smooths the cursor path. QuickTime cannot do this.

Does Slipreel run on my Mac like QuickTime?

Yes. Slipreel runs on macOS 13 or later, Apple Silicon or Intel, and everything runs locally on your Mac.
