The ScreenFlow alternative that edits itself.

ScreenFlow is a full manual video editor. Slipreel gives you a polished demo automatically — auto-zoom, smoothed cursor, device frames, and captions — for a lower one-time price, without a timeline to wrangle.

ScreenFlow is a powerful, mature screen recorder and multi-track video editor for the Mac — if you want to hand-edit, animate, and mix a video, it does far more than Slipreel. Slipreel takes the opposite approach: it produces the polished result automatically. It detects your clicks and zooms in, smooths the cursor, wraps the app in a device frame, and transcribes captions on-device — so a clean demo falls out without keyframing anything.

Here is how they compare, and when ScreenFlow's depth is worth it.

Slipreel vs ScreenFlow: common questions

Is Slipreel a good ScreenFlow alternative?

Yes, for product demos. Slipreel produces a polished result automatically — auto-zoom, a smoothed cursor, device frames, and on-device captions — for a lower one-time price. ScreenFlow is the better pick if you need a full multi-track video editor with transitions, animations, and ProRes export.

Does Slipreel have automatic zoom that ScreenFlow doesn't?

Yes. Slipreel detects your clicks and zooms in automatically. In ScreenFlow you add and keyframe each zoom by hand on the timeline.

Is Slipreel cheaper than ScreenFlow?

Yes. Slipreel is $129 once, with a year of updates included. ScreenFlow is $199 as a one-time purchase, and its stock media library is a further $99 per year.

Can Slipreel edit like ScreenFlow?

Slipreel has focused timeline editing — cut at the playhead, with per-slice trim and speed — but it is not a full multi-track editor. For transitions, text and motion animations, nested clips, and ProRes, ScreenFlow does far more.

Do Slipreel's captions require manual work like ScreenFlow's?

No. Slipreel transcribes on-device automatically, then lets you edit the text and burn it in on export. It runs on your Mac and your recording is never uploaded.

Does Slipreel run on Windows?

No. Slipreel is macOS 13 or later, on Apple Silicon or Intel — the same Mac-only footprint as ScreenFlow.
